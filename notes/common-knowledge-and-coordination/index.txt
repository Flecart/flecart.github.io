28:T28bf2,<h2 id="common-knowledge-and-coordination">Common Knowledge and Coordination<a class="anchor" aria-hidden="true" hidden href="#common-knowledge-and-coordination">#</a></h2>
<p>Notes from a tutoring session, taken about halfway through the material. The chain is: two generals <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.3669em;"></span><span class="mrel">→</span></span></span></span> partitions and common knowledge <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.3669em;"></span><span class="mrel">→</span></span></span></span> Halpern–Moses <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.3669em;"></span><span class="mrel">→</span></span></span></span> common <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.625em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">p</span></span></span></span>-belief <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.3669em;"></span><span class="mrel">→</span></span></span></span> email game <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.3669em;"></span><span class="mrel">→</span></span></span></span> global games <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.3669em;"></span><span class="mrel">→</span></span></span></span> public vs private signals. Here we revise it at master's level; the one mechanism to remember is that <em>every result is the same move repeated</em>: find the world someone cannot rule out, look at what is true there, and chain.</p>

<h3 id="1-two-generals">1. Two generals<a class="anchor" aria-hidden="true" hidden href="#1-two-generals">#</a></h3>
<p>Two generals win only if they attack together; a lone attacker is destroyed. Messengers may be captured, and a sender never learns whether a message arrived unless he hears back.</p>
<h4 id="each-message-adds-one-level-and-leaves-one-gap">Each message adds one level and leaves one gap<a class="anchor" aria-hidden="true" hidden href="#each-message-adds-one-level-and-leaves-one-gap">#</a></h4>
<p>Let <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.625em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">p</span></span></span></span> = "A's plan is to attack at dawn". After <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.4306em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">n</span></span></span></span> delivered messages the deepest true formula has <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.4306em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">n</span></span></span></span> knowledge operators, and the <strong>sender of message <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.4306em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">n</span></span></span></span></strong> is the one who cannot tell "delivered" from "lost".</p>

<h4 id="impossibility-of-a-fixed-length-protocol">Impossibility of a fixed-length protocol<a class="anchor" aria-hidden="true" hidden href="#impossibility-of-a-fixed-length-protocol">#</a></h4>
<p>Claim to refute: "exchange exactly <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.4306em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">n</span></span></span></span> messages; attack iff all <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.4306em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">n</span></span></span></span> arrive". The sender of message <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.4306em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">n</span></span></span></span> sees the same thing whether it arrived or not, so he must choose the same action in both worlds.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>He attacks in both</strong>: in the world where message <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.4306em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">n</span></span></span></span> was lost, the other general holds, so he attacks alone.</li>
<li><strong>He holds in both</strong>: in the world where everything arrived, the other general attacks alone.</li>
</ul>
<p>Either way the protocol cannot be followed. Repeat for <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6667em;vertical-align:-0.0833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">n</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2222em;"></span><span class="mbin">−</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2222em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">1</span></span></span></span>, <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6667em;vertical-align:-0.0833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">n</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2222em;"></span><span class="mbin">−</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2222em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">2</span></span></span></span>, ... and no fixed <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.4306em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">n</span></span></span></span> works.</p>
<h4 id="why-ordinary-communication-still-works">Why ordinary communication still works<a class="anchor" aria-hidden="true" hidden href="#why-ordinary-communication-still-works">#</a></h4>
<p>The theorem is about actions that are safe only if taken <strong>jointly</strong>. A chat, or a TCP handshake, never needs that: each step is safe on one-sided knowledge and a lost message costs a retry.</p>

<div class="callout" data-callout="question">
<div class="callout-title"><span class="callout-icon" aria-hidden="true"><svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" viewBox="0 0 24 24" width="18" height="18" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round"><circle cx="12" cy="12" r="10"></circle><path d="M9.09 9a3 3 0 0 1 5.83 1c0 2-3 3-3 3"></path><path d="M12 17h.01"></path></svg></span><span class="callout-title-inner">Why not 0.99?</span></div>
<div class="callout-content"><p>0.99 answers "what is the chance my message gets through?". The block asks "given that I heard nothing back, what is the chance it got through?". </div>
<p>The same ratio holds at every block along the chain, from either side.</p>

<h3 id="5-the-email-game">5. The email game<a class="anchor" aria-hidden="true" hidden href="#5-the-email-game">#</a></h3>
<p>Ariel Rubinstein's "The Electronic Mail Game: Strategic Behavior Under 'Almost Common Knowledge'" (American Economic Review, 1989).</p>
<h4 id="setup-in-generals-terms">Setup (in generals' terms)<a class="anchor" aria-hidden="true" hidden href="#setup-in-generals-terms">#</a></h4>
<p>The enemy is weak with probability <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.7335em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0359em;">q</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span><span class="mrel">&#x3C;</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:1em;vertical-align:-0.25em;"></span><span class="mord">1/2</span></span></span></span>; only A observes it. Holding pays 0. A joint attack on a weak enemy pays <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.109em;">M</span></span></span></span> each. Attacking alone, or attacking a strong enemy, costs the attacker <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">L</span></span></span></span>, with <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.7224em;vertical-align:-0.0391em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">L</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span><span class="mrel">></span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.7224em;vertical-align:-0.0391em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.109em;">M</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span><span class="mrel">></span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">0</span></span></span></span>. If the enemy is weak, A's machine sends a message and every receipt is automatically acknowledged, each lost with probability <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.4306em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">ε</span></span></span></span>. Each general sees only his own machine's count.</p>

<h3 id="6-global-games">6. Global games<a class="anchor" aria-hidden="true" hidden href="#6-global-games">#</a></h3>
<h4 id="risk-dominance">Risk dominance<a class="anchor" aria-hidden="true" hidden href="#risk-dominance">#</a></h4>
<blockquote>
<p>An equilibrium is <strong>risk dominant</strong> when its action is the best response to an opponent who plays each action with probability one half.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Stag hunt with payoffs (Stag, Stag) <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.3669em;"></span><span class="mrel">=</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.8389em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord">4</span><span class="mpunct">,</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.1667em;"></span><span class="mord">4</span></span></span></span>; (Stag, Hare) <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.3669em;"></span><span class="mrel">=</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.8389em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord">0</span><span class="mpunct">,</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.1667em;"></span><span class="mord">3</span></span></span></span>; (Hare, Hare) <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.3669em;"></span><span class="mrel">=</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.8389em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord">3</span><span class="mpunct">,</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.1667em;"></span><span class="mord">3</span></span></span></span>: against 50/50, Stag pays 2 and Hare pays 3, so (Hare, Hare) is risk dominant although (Stag, Stag) is payoff dominant. Stag is a best response only with belief <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.8304em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0359em;">q</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span><span class="mrel">≥</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:1em;vertical-align:-0.25em;"></span><span class="mord">3/4</span></span></span></span> on Stag.</p>
<p>Warning: "the action that always gives 3" is the <strong>maximin</strong> criterion. It coincides with risk dominance here but is a different test.</p>
<h4 id="definition-of-a-global-game">Definition of a global game<a class="anchor" aria-hidden="true" hidden href="#definition-of-a-global-game">#</a></h4>

<h4 id="a-noisy-public-signal-can-do-harm">A noisy public signal can do harm<a class="anchor" aria-hidden="true" hidden href="#a-noisy-public-signal-can-do-harm">#</a></h4>
<ul>
<li>Private errors are independent and wash out across a population.</li>
<li>The error in <span class="katex"><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.625em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0359em;">y</span></span></span></span> is common to everyone, does not wash out, and by the previous point is leaned on more than its accuracy warrants.</li>
<li>So adding a mediocre public signal to good private information moves everyone toward the same mistake. More public precision helps only once it is high enough relative to private precision (exact threshold not recalled).</li>
</ul>

<h4 id="law-as-a-public-signal">Law as a public signal<a class="anchor" aria-hidden="true" hidden href="#law-as-a-public-signal">#</a></h4>
<p>A published law is built to be a self-evident event: everyone sees it and sees that everyone sees it. On the belief-based reading of institutions, where a law changes beliefs and not the physical rules of the game, this gives a concrete model of <em>how</em> it acts: as a public signal whose force comes from being common knowledge, on top of anything it says about the world or about enforcement. The drawbacks carry over too: overreaction to announcements, shared errors, and loss of predictability when the announcement is very precise. Compare David Lewis's conventions and focal points in Thomas Schelling's <em>The Strategy of Conflict</em>.</p>
